Syracuse University acquisitions, September 2016–September 2026

Evidence checked through September 7, 2026. Integrated as the detailed acquisition account supporting the illustrated briefing.

The bounded search identifies 15 acquisition events covering 18 site records: 14 purchases and one gift. These are transaction groupings, not a count of deeds, buildings, or current tax parcels. Four Dellas parcels share one purchase; two deeds at 153 Marshall concern the same address; the acquired extent at 195 Ainsley remains unresolved. One additional small-land candidate is kept outside the confirmed count.

The acquisitions extend beyond the familiar apartment building, hospital education center and Marshall Street shops. Newly identified additions to the earlier account are 206 Walnut Place, 410–418 South Crouse Avenue, 813 Comstock Avenue, 805–807 Walnut Avenue, 210 Walnut Place, 727 Comstock Avenue, an interest at 195 Ainsley Drive, and the gift of 300 Waverly Avenue. They add group residences, a clinical building, vacant land, a former single-family house, and a historic sorority house to the acquisition story.

This is a deliberately broad public-record search, not a certification that every SU-related entity or every legal interest has been found. The strongest systematic coverage is the City of Syracuse assessment rolls and the Onondaga County direct-name land index. The limits below are part of the findings.

Reading the register

Sale date below means the city's municipal sale entry, not an independently inspected closing document. The companion JSON separately records county filing dates, announcements and unknown agreement/closing dates. Dollar amounts are reported consideration unless explicitly labelled otherwise. Deed locators are municipal book/page or county year/instrument references. Municipal records are transcriptions; county index entries were inspected, but paid deed images and transfer-tax attachments were not obtained.

No reliable asking price or property-specific appraisal was located for these transactions. Assessments appear separately with their year. They are not actual market worth. The municipal “Full Market Value” field is an administrative equalization calculation (2026 rate 52%), not an independent appraisal; it is deliberately not used to declare overpayment. There is no defensible portfolio-price allocation or matched comparable-sale valuation in this register.

Houses and small land purchases

Locate on the briefing map ↗206 Walnut Place is a house used for group residence, not a newly constructed dormitory. SU acquired it from the municipally named New York Delta Assoc for $1.25 million, with a September 29, 2016 sale entry and October 20 filing. Later SU housing documentation identifies residential use. The specific purchase motive is not established, and this register does not claim that 2016 was the first SU ownership in the building's entire history. 2026 assessment: $718,000. Municipal record; SU residence documentation.

Locate on the briefing map ↗813 Comstock Avenue, extending toward Ostrom Alley, was a single-family house when SU bought it from CRE 44 LLC for $402,500 on November 30, 2020; the deed was filed December 2. It is now classified as vacant residential land. A city demolition permit was issued May 4, 2021; the exact demolition date is not established here. The purchase motive remains unknown. An older seller named “SU GC LLC” is not assumed to be university-affiliated. 2026 assessment: $80,000, reflecting a different physical condition from the house purchased in 2020. Municipal history; permit history.

Locate on the briefing map ↗805–807 Walnut Avenue, at Marshall Street, is a vacant commercial-land parcel. SU purchased it from Walsig House Corporation for $900,000, municipal date March 31, 2021, recorded April 1. It contains 17,905 square feet in the municipal record. No specific building project or acquisition rationale was established. Adjacency alone does not prove a redevelopment commitment. 2026 assessment: $268,000. Municipal record.

Locate on the briefing map ↗210 Walnut Place, at Marshall Street, is a former fraternity/group residence associated with Phi Delta Theta. The sale entry is $1.975 million, August 11, 2021, also the filing date. The municipal seller is 210 Walnut Place Associates LLC, while the county index says 201 Walnut Place Associates LLC; the legal spelling remains to be checked against the instrument. SU's building-supervisor directory includes the property, but detailed current occupancy and the original purchase rationale are unknown. 2026 assessment: $1 million. Municipal record; county index, 2021-00038373; chapter history; SU directory.

Locate on the briefing map ↗727 Comstock Avenue is the historic Estabrook house, subsequently occupied by fraternity chapters including Zeta Psi, Phi Gamma Delta/Fiji and Sigma Alpha Mu. SU purchased it from the Syracuse Association of Zeta Psi for $3.14 million, municipal date September 30, 2022, recorded October 3. A 2024 university redevelopment proposal would have demolished it for an Ostrom-area dormitory; that proposal was withdrawn in August, and the council protected the property in October. This establishes a later proposed use, not a documented motive at the 2022 closing. Detailed present occupancy remains unverified. 2026 assessment: $750,000. Municipal record; city application; protection report.

Locate on the briefing map ↗300 Waverly Avenue, municipally “300 Waverly Avenue Rear,” is the Upsilon Alpha Chapter House, formerly Chi Omega's home and later leased to Phi Sigma Sigma. Upsilon Alpha Alumnae of Syracuse, NY Inc. gave it to SU in 2025. The municipal entry is August 15, the deed and restrictive covenant were filed August 18, and SU announced the gift that day. The nominal $1 is not a market purchase price. The stated purpose is meeting and reunion space for Greek organizations, preserving the donor's legacy and supporting Student Experience. The announcement describes preservation covenants; their full legal terms were not inspected. 2026 assessment: $400,000; gift appraisal unknown. SU gift announcement; county instruments 2025-00026371 and 26372.

Clinical, academic and apartment buildings

Locate on the briefing map ↗410–418 South Crouse Avenue was Crouse's chemical-dependency outpatient center. SU bought the building for $3.25 million from Crouse Health Hospital Inc., municipal date June 14, 2018, recorded June 18. SU's archives independently identify the purchase; Crouse moved out in 2021 and renovation followed in 2021–22. Subsequent university contact information places offices there. Specific purchase-time intent is unknown. The original parcel 048.-13-07.0 is historical; the later composite 048.-13-02.3 should not be treated as this building alone. 2019 standalone assessment: $2,211,200; no present building-only assessment is assigned. SU building history; municipal sale; later office use.

The Marshall, now Milton Hall, is the 287-bed mixed-use apartment building at 727 South Crouse Avenue. It is entirely different from 727 Comstock Avenue. The municipal purchase entry is $69,444,444, July 9, 2021, from The Marshall 727 LLC; county filing occurred July 22. The audited FY2021 report gives the compatible rounded figure $69.4 million, paid from operating cash. The developer's July 13 announcement identifies Aptitude's sale, but developer and legal grantor names should remain distinct. FY2022 describes student apartments; the later housing strategy brought it into sophomore housing in fall 2024. The acquisition parcel .14.1 became current .14.2. 2026 assessment: $24,228,000, not an appraisal supporting a premium calculation. historical sale record; FY2021 report, note 14; FY2022 report; Milton history.

Marley Education Center, now commonly 761 Irving Avenue, is the former nursing education building also encountered as 765 Irving and the municipal 761–787 Irving/University Place property. SU purchased it from Crouse Health Systems Inc. for $34 million, municipal date December 22, 2021, recorded December 23. The FY2022 report corroborates December 2021 and describes academic uses. A later archive's “2022” label should not overwrite those transaction records. Psychology's 2024 consolidation is documented later reuse, not proof that this exact departmental move drove the original acquisition. 2026 assessment: $20 million. Municipal record; FY2022 report; psychology move.

Retail and hotel properties

The Dellas portfolio comprises four South Crouse parcels: 732–740, 742, 746 and 802. DB Real Estate Crouse LLC transferred them to SU in one $12 million transaction, municipal date July 21, 2022, recorded July 26. Each municipal record repeats the same portfolio consideration and links the other parcels. It must be counted once. The portfolio includes storefront properties and a small parking/access parcel; Varsity's property is at 802. The restaurant businesses were not themselves the real-estate acquisition. Exact tenant footprints should not be inferred from the four tax addresses. The sources establish ownership expansion in the commercial district, but not a parcel-specific redevelopment decision. 2026 assessments respectively: $810,000; $25,000; $661,000; $529,000. Municipal portfolio links; business-owner account.

737 South Crouse Avenue, also the 101 Marshall Street corner, is another mixed-use storefront property. SU bought it from 101 Marshall LLC for $5.85 million, municipal date July 5, 2023, filed July 7. A specific purchase rationale and detailed current tenant roster were not established. 2026 assessment: $815,000. Municipal record.

Locate on the briefing map ↗Hotel Skyler, at 601 South Crouse Avenue, is the 58-room hotel acquired for $10.05 million from Hotel Skyler LLC, municipal date March 27, 2024. SU announced the purchase March 28, describing continued hotel use and visitor accommodation as the Sheraton became student housing. The municipal owner is 601 South Crouse Avenue LLC. SU's FY2024 audited note 2(n) explicitly identifies that company as wholly owned by SU, resolving the ownership bridge; FY2025 repeats it. The second municipal name, Skyler Mgmnt Company LLC, is not a reason to leave university ownership unresolved. The announcement's forward-looking transfer language differs from the municipal date; no separate closing document was inspected. 2026 assessment: $4.03 million. The audited note describing an unnamed property-acquisition loan is not allocated to Skyler. SU announcement; FY2024 report, PDF p.27; municipal record.

Locate on the briefing map ↗153 Marshall Street and Locate on the briefing map ↗159–163 Marshall Street are two commercial properties acquired in February 2025. The city records $3.7 million from Marshall Redfield LLC and $6.8 million from Marshall Street II LLC, both dated February 28. These sum to $10.5 million. NCC News reported $14.2 million combined and quoted SU's position that there were no immediate changes planned. 2026 assessments: $680,000 and $760,000. 153 municipal record; 159–163 municipal record; NCC report.

The county index adds an essential qualification: three deeds were filed March 3, 2025—2025-00006646 ($6.8 million), 2025-00006647 ($3.7 million), and 2025-00006649 ($3.7 million). The third names Jackson Family Trust → Syracuse University at 153 Marshall Street. Adding all three reproduces $14.2 million. This explains the numerical discrepancy but does not prove whether the third amount is additional payment for a separate interest or the same consideration repeated in another deed. Neither sum is certified here as total economic consideration. The additional deed belongs under 153 Marshall, not a newly invented third building. Jackson deed index details.

Adjacent-context land: Ainsley

195 Ainsley Drive is a former parking/vacant-land property in the Ainsley/Outer Comstock context, outside the agreed core. The county index confirms 185 Ainsley Drive LLC → Syracuse University, $200,000, filed July 27, 2023, instrument 2023-00023915. The closing date is unknown. SU had petitioned to bring the property into its planned institutional subdistrict; this supports an inference of institutional land assembly, but no specific operational project is established. Deed index; council packet, PDF pp.10 and 23.

Do not map the entire old 195 Ainsley parcel as acquired. The 2023 parent was 061.-02-25.0, classified as parking with a $168,200 whole-parcel assessment. The smaller current 061.-02-25.1 remains privately owned. The evidence is consistent with a partial conveyance and possible combination with neighboring SU land, but the acquired polygon and successor parcel are not conclusively established. Neither that old assessment nor the private remainder's assessment is assigned to SU's interest. The JSON carries an explicit extent limitation and no substitute centroid. Historical roll; current private remainder.

What the acquisitions show

The evidence supports three distinct activities. First, SU acquired large buildings for institutional reuse: student housing at the Marshall and academic space at Marley. Second, it accumulated commercial properties around South Crouse and Marshall while existing retail activity continued. Third, it acquired smaller houses and land parcels whose original motives are often less publicly documented. The 2025 Waverly gift adds a donor-led preservation and student-life purpose rather than a purchase strategy. Skyler has the clearest explicit connection between an acquisition and another building's change of use.

The chronology does not justify treating every parcel as part of one announced redevelopment scheme. Later demolition proposals, department moves and planning boundaries are evidence of later decisions. They are not substitutes for purchase-time intent. Likewise, sale prices exceeding tax assessments establish neither overpayment nor an independently measured “strategic premium.” Internal valuations, development rights, retained interests, lease obligations and closing allocations remain largely unavailable.

Its inherited history points to parent .01.1 and a 2010 deed, not a demonstrated 2017/18 purchase. It remains an unresolved candidate, excluded from confirmed counts. 2026 assessment: $19,800, with acquisition date and consideration unknown. A second-owner entry for SU at 917 Madison Street #115 in 2019–20 likewise does not establish a purchase; the current record identifies private ownership and an old sale. Genesee record; Madison record.

The county index also contains a December 27, 2021 right of first refusal, instrument 2021-00062276, naming SU and St Thomas More Foundation, southeast block 375, lots 7, 9, 11, 13 and 14. Without its text, the direction, conditions, duration and present enforceability are unknown. It is recorded separately as a legal-right lead, not a completed acquisition or a commitment to buy. No explicit future purchase commitment was established. Planning districts alone are not purchase intent. County index.

Coverage completed and limits

The search combined broad year-specific purchase/acquisition queries across 2016–2026 and property types; SU news, archives and audited reports; local reporting and seller/broker leads; eleven city final assessment rolls; current and historical municipal sales records; and the county's free land-record index. Original evidence informed leads; quarantined preliminary narratives were not read.

  • City roll screen completed: primary or secondary owner containing “SYRACUSE UNIV,” case-insensitive, 2016–2026. Returned counts were 112, 113, 114, 115, 114, 116, 118, 122, 121, 112 and 110. All observed additions were investigated. Changes at Schine/Waverly, Euclid, College Place, University Avenue, Ostrom, Van Buren and campus parcels were screened for renumbering rather than automatically counted as purchases. Ainsley demonstrates why roll comparison alone is insufficient: a purchase can disappear into a reconfigured parcel.
  • County direct-name screen completed: “SYRACUSE UNIV” prefix, both parties, land records, descending date. The first 300 entries extended from August 2026 into 1994, covering the requested period without further pagination. The entered date filter did not persist; the actual dates were screened. Relevant incoming deed rows were reconciled to this register. Credit-union entries, mortgage assignments, outbound state appropriations, and routine easements/agreements were distinguished from fee acquisitions. This is not a survey of every easement's legal effect.
  • Financial and public-source screen completed within its stated limits: FY2017–FY2025 acquisition-related text and notes, institutional acquisition histories, targeted property and asking-price searches. Consolidated reports are not complete parcel schedules. Construction spending, an unnamed acquisition-financing note, leases and investment-fund holdings were not converted into invented property purchases. Older SU assets and new buildings on already owned land were excluded from the acquisition count.
  • Not exhausted: all subsidiary/affiliate names, properties outside Onondaga County, foreign programs, unrecorded or temporarily held interests, and full deed/transfer-tax documentation. The Skyler subsidiary is affirmatively connected through audited statements; that does not prove every holding entity was found. The county's paid image route was not used. Direct public-page blocks and the exhausted Firecrawl route were not repeatedly retried.
  • Remaining material gaps: 153 Marshall's total economic consideration; the exact Ainsley conveyance boundary; 818 Genesee's acquisition mechanism; asking prices, appraisals and many purchase motives. The period stops September 7, 2026, with municipal data observed uploaded September 4. Later September events cannot yet be covered.

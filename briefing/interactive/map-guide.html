A map for reading the precedent study

These maps connect places with the plans, decisions and investments discussed in the briefing. They are a location reference for the team and its architectural drawings, not a survey or a representation of every proposed future building.

Find a place, then follow its story

  • Click a building, parcel or corridor to open its explanation. Use “Read” to return to the relevant briefing passage.
  • Click a linked place name in the prose to select it on that chapter’s map. “Locate these places” focuses the places mentioned in a discussion.
  • Use the overall map to see connections across chapters. Filter by subject, search an address or select a name. Housing context is a separate optional layer.
  • Download an SVG of the visible map for a readable drawing reference, or the geometry for use in mapping software. The SVG does not include the street-tile background; retain the source notes when using it.

Different boundaries mean different things

LayerMeaning
Municipal neighborhoodsCity of Syracuse’s 2017 neighborhood layer. The selected core is the agreed approximately 2.25-square-mile study footprint. Additional surrounding neighborhoods are included by geographic relevance. A municipal neighborhood does not define a university campus, a business district or an institution’s holdings.
Named orientation areasMedical institutions, the academic core, ESF, commercial sites and East Adams housing are grouped to make the study legible. Their envelopes join selected mapped sites and are explicitly labelled “orientation area”; they are not official or surveyed district boundaries.
Campus housingNamed Main/North Campus residences and the South Campus Skyhalls. This locates housing clusters; it is not a complete housing inventory, current occupancy count or acquisition list. South Campus apartments extend beyond the Skyhalls. The municipal South Campus polygon is a different geographic reference.
Site outlinesCity parcel polygons where the site match is supported; otherwise named building or parking-surface outlines. Every feature states its source and limitations. Historical building outlines used to locate redevelopment are explicitly identified. A shaded parcel does not establish a future building envelope, acquisition intention or business ownership.
CorridorsExisting mapped street centerlines locate transport and planning discussions. They are not adopted future alignments, staging plans or entire project boundaries. Dashed treatments identify proposal/discussion references.

The study area

One university core, three parts, read parcel by parcel

The four census tracts around Syracuse University — the Marshall Street commercial spine, the University Hill institutional district, and the residential University Neighborhood — analyzed over the City of Syracuse 2025 Q3 parcel roll and mapped on live satellite imagery.
  • Marshall Street — the commercial Main Street; SU has been buying the frontage.
  • University Hill — SU, SUNY Upstate, Crouse, the VA; the institutional core west to the I-81 line.
  • University Neighborhood — the subdivided-house student rental district, ~99% privately owned.

Finding 1 · Ownership

Institutional ownership is decisive.

Syracuse University owns about 69% of the tight campus core's assessed value and roughly 42% across the four study tracts, and is largely property-tax-exempt; the city receives a negotiated SIDA PILOT (~$4.26M, FY2024) rather than tax. About 53% of the citywide property base is exempt.

Finding 2 · Zoning

The zoning was rewritten to a form-based code.

ReZone (adopted June 2023) replaced Euclidean zoning with form/character-based mixed-use districts, reduced or removed parking minimums in several cases, and set the first mandatory affordability requirement for a large Upstate city (10% for 20–75-unit projects).
  • Euclidean use-zoning → form/character-based mixed-use (MX) districts (adopted June 2023).
  • Parking minimums reduced or removed in several districts and with the affordability set-aside.
  • First mandatory affordability rule for a large Upstate city — 10% for 20–75-unit projects.

Finding 3 · The market

The housing market runs on three ownership tiers.

  • Local landlords — subdivided 1–3-family houses (~47% of core parcels), low owner-occupancy, high code-violation counts.
  • National capital — a few purpose-built student-housing towers (Landmark, American Campus, BVK/Peak) holding a large share of value.
  • The university — dorms, ground leases, and a growing hold on the commercial spine.

Finding 4 · External shock

An external shock is driving the market.

Amid Micron's ~$100B fab planned ~20 miles away, county for-sale inventory fell roughly 75–83%, and University Hill is now the most expensive neighborhood in the city (~$2,503/mo).

Finding 5 · Governance

Governance is fragmented.

There is no single district manager. A thin Crouse-Marshall BID, the University Hill Corporation, the University Neighborhood Preservation Association, and SU's community-relations office share the field, split physically by the I-81 town-gown line.
  • No single district manager with sustained capacity.
  • A thin Crouse-Marshall BID, the University Hill Corporation, UNPA, and SU's community-relations office.
  • The I-81 viaduct is a near-literal town-gown line — institutions east, high-poverty city west.

Context · Section 3

A flat-enrollment university in a shrinking city

SU enrollment has held near 22,000 for a decade while the City of Syracuse has declined from its 1950 peak. The university-and-hospital economy is the region's anchor.

Synthesis · The university's plan

How Syracuse University planned the district — and what changed

Its own master plan is a statement of intent. The 2017 Campus Framework set the strategy; the 2023 Refresh audited it and, in places, reversed it.
  • Planned · 2017 — relocate South Campus underclass housing to Main; build a “Campus-City Community” along University Avenue.
  • Built · 2019–2024 — Milton Hall, Orange Hall, and 20+ projects delivered; two more halls announced (700 Ostrom; Comstock/Waverly).
  • Revised · 2023 — keep and reinvest in South Campus, rely on private housing near Main, and shelve the University Avenue concept.

Synthesis · Precedent

Implications for Collegetown

Eight inferences drawn from the Syracuse evidence. Each is marked as an inference — a precedent-based hypothesis to test against Collegetown's own conditions, not a fact about Collegetown.
Syracuse Precedent · M. Kodsi
10
Inference   Zoning & code · §2

A form-based code rewrite is the main lever on density and affordability

The zoning code sets the district's built form. Syracuse shows a city can replace Euclidean zoning with a form-based mixed-use system that permits mid-rise by-right and carries a mandatory inclusionary requirement. A code rewrite, rather than project-by-project variances, is therefore the main tool available for changing both density and affordability.
Where it holds / breaks: Collegetown is governed by the City of Ithaca's code, not Syracuse's. The transferable lesson is the mechanism (form-based + inclusionary), not the specific district parameters, which reflect Syracuse's market and politics.
Syracuse Precedent · M. Kodsi
11
Inference   Institutional ownership · §7

A dominant tax-exempt university reshapes the municipal tax base

Where one university owns most of the core's assessed value, the municipal tax base is correspondingly narrow, the city relies on a negotiated PILOT or voluntary payment rather than property tax, and the exemption becomes a standing fiscal grievance. Expect the same dynamics around Cornell's footprint in Collegetown.
Where it holds / breaks: The 69% figure is boundary-dependent (42%–70.9% depending on how the core is drawn). Cornell/Ithaca's exemption share and any PILOT arrangements differ and must be checked directly — this identifies the pattern to test, not Collegetown's numbers.
Syracuse Precedent · M. Kodsi

Inference   Real estate & capital · §7

National capital enters a tight market and concentrates value in a few towers

Once a college-town rental market tightens, out-of-state purpose-built student housing (PBSH) capital moves in. A handful of towers end up holding a disproportionate share of value and reset the rent structure onto a per-bed basis.
Where it holds / breaks: This is a market-stage observation. Whether Collegetown is at the same point depends on its current development pipeline and remaining land — a tightening market is the precondition, not a guarantee.
Syracuse Precedent · M. Kodsi
13
Inference   Real estate & housing · §7

The house stock consolidates into a low-owner-occupancy, code-violation-heavy submarket

The 1–3-family houses around a campus tend to consolidate under a few landlords, fall to low owner-occupancy, and carry high code-violation counts. This is a common condition of student-neighborhood housing, not specific to Syracuse.
Where it holds / breaks: Owner-occupancy here is a mailing-address proxy, and violation counts reflect enforcement activity as well as conditions. Collegetown's exact figures need its own parcel roll and code data, but the direction is well-established.

Inference   Regional economy · §10

A regional demand shock can overwhelm the college-town market

A college town's housing market is not insulated from its region. A nearby megaproject can create demand that exceeds student demand and pushes up rents across the area.
Where it holds / breaks: Co-movement is not proof of causation, and Ithaca has no equivalent megaproject on the horizon. The transferable point is a college town's exposure to regional demand, not Micron specifically.

Inference   Governance & retail · §4

The university may vertically integrate its commercial Main Street

A university with the balance sheet to do so may buy up its commercial Main Street, shifting control of college-town retail from independent merchants to the institution.
Where it holds / breaks: This is an active, incomplete process. How much SU ends up owning, and to what use, is not yet known — watch it as an unfolding pattern rather than a settled outcome.
Syracuse Precedent · M. Kodsi
16
Inference   University policy · §7

University housing policy sets the off-campus market's timing and volume

A residency requirement directly controls off-campus demand. Requiring underclassmen to live on campus concentrates that demand into the upper years, which sets how much and when the private market absorbs.
Where it holds / breaks: Cornell's own residency rules (e.g., the North Campus requirement) differ. The mechanism transfers — policy sets the demand curve — but the specific timing and split do not.
Syracuse Precedent · M. Kodsi
17
Inference   Governance & boundaries · §4

Governance is layered, and a hard town-gown edge can be physical

College-town governance is rarely one body. Expect a small BID, a residents' preservation association, the university's community-relations office, and city planning to divide responsibility — and a physical barrier can turn the town-gown boundary into a literal one.
Where it holds / breaks: Collegetown's specific governance actors and any physical edges are its own. This names the categories to look for — district manager, residents' org, university liaison, hard boundary — not the specific bodies.
